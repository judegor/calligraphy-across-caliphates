<div class="info-block">
  <h3>Historical Context</h3>
  <p>
    <strong>Thuluth</strong> (Arabic <em>thuluth</em>, “one-third”) was developed in the tenth century under Abbasid reforms and systematized by Ibn Muqla. Its name refers to the proportion of the pen nib used. Thuluth was designed as a display script—majestic, fluid, and large-scale—making it ideal for monumental inscriptions.
  </p>
</div>

<div class="info-block">
  <h3>Visual Characteristics</h3>
  <ul>
    <li>Long verticals, broad curves, dramatic contrast of line weight</li>
    <li>Highly ornamental; challenging to execute well</li>
    <li>Used primarily for architecture, headings, and imperial proclamations</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<blockquote class="academic-quote">
  “Thuluth was the most majestic and expressive of the scripts and, as a result, the most difficult to write well. It was typically reserved for important texts… where its large letters and decorative flourishes were especially appropriate.”
  <br><span class="quote-author">— Sheila S. Blair, <em>Islamic Calligraphy</em>, p. 494</span>
</blockquote>
